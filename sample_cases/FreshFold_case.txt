FreshFold: A laundry startup under price attack

FreshFold is a Hyderabad-based laundry subscription service founded in 2022 by Kavya Reddy. Customers book pickups on an app; clothes are washed, ironed and returned within 48 hours. FreshFold runs two processing hubs and a fleet of 60 delivery riders.

The numbers. FreshFold has 18,000 paying subscribers. The standard plan costs Rs 899 per month for up to 25 garments. Gross margin is 34%. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is about Rs 1,400, mostly from social-media ads and referral credits. Monthly churn is 9%, up from 6% a year ago. Exit surveys say the top reasons for leaving are "too expensive" (41%), "late deliveries" (27%) and "moving out of the city" (18%). Hub utilization is 68%.

The threat. QuickPress, a new competitor, raised USD 20 million in March 2026 and launched a Rs 499 plan for 20 garments in Hyderabad and Bengaluru. Industry observers believe QuickPress is losing money on every order to grab market share. FreshFold lost about 1,100 subscribers in the two months after the launch.

Options on the table.
1. Match the price: launch a Rs 499 plan. The CFO estimates gross margin on that plan would be close to zero unless hub utilization rises above 90%.
2. Go premium: keep prices, add same-day delivery, stain treatment and eco-friendly detergents, and target families in gated communities who value reliability.
3. Pivot to B2B: sell bulk contracts to student hostels, PG accommodations and mid-size hotels. A pilot with three hostels produced Rs 4.2 lakh monthly revenue at a 29% gross margin, with almost no acquisition cost, but payments arrived 45 to 60 days late.

FreshFold has nine months of cash left. Its investors are split: one says "never fight a funded competitor on price", another says "if you lose share now you will never get it back". Kavya must present a plan to the board next month.
